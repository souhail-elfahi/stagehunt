# Write like a person

Write like a student explaining their own project to a friend: clear, specific, a bit informal, honest about limits.

Do:
- Plain words and short sentences. Vary the length.
- Concrete facts and numbers instead of adjectives ("checks 5 times a day", not "powerful automation").
- Say why before how, in one sentence.
- First person where it fits: "I built this because I was applying blind."
- Admit limits plainly: "Windows only for now."
- One idea per paragraph. Headings that say what's below them.

Don't:
- Hype words: seamless, powerful, robust, cutting-edge, leverage, unlock, empower, revolutionize, game-changer, supercharge, streamline, delve, elevate.
- Openers like "In today's fast-paced world", "Whether you're a… or a…", "Let's dive in".
- Closing summaries that repeat the text ("In conclusion…", "Happy job hunting!").
- Lists of three adjectives, rhetorical questions, exclamation marks everywhere.
- Emoji in every heading (one or two in the whole README at most).
- Long dashes to glue sentences together: use a full stop.
- Bold words in the middle of sentences.

In French also avoid: "n'hésitez pas", "dans un monde où", "véritable", "incontournable", "booster", "au cœur de", needless anglicisms.

Before finishing, reread the text and cut any sentence that would still be true for any other project.
